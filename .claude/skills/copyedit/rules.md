# Copyedit rules

The reader is a recruiter who has read hundreds of applications this week and recognises the template. Write text that could only be about this person and this job, in plain sentences a person would say out loud.

Each rule below names a pattern to find and what to write in its place. The examples are English; the patterns are not. In any other language, look for that language's own form of each pattern: its stock application phrases, its officialese, its buzzwords. Find them natively, rather than translating the English examples and searching for those.

A voice file's Notes override these rules where they conflict: it is the author's own voice.

## Structure

- **Contrast framing.** "Not just X, but Y", "It's not about X, it's about Y", "more than X". Write Y on its own.
- **Run-ups and reveals.** "Here's the thing:", "The result?", "What drew me in was simple:". Put the point first.
- **Forced triads.** Three adjectives, three clauses, three examples chosen for rhythm. Use the number the content has, often one or two.
- **-ing riders.** "…, ensuring reliability", "…, highlighting my commitment to quality". Make the effect its own sentence with a fact, or cut it.
- **Inflated significance.** "pivotal", "a testament to", "played a key role in shaping". Say what happened, with its number.
- **Closers and sayings.** A short last line that sums up or sounds wise: "That's what drives me.", "Quality is a habit." End on the last fact or the request.
- **Avoiding "is" and "has".** "serves as", "stands as", "boasts". Write "is" or "has".
- **Synonym cycling.** Pick one word for a thing and repeat it.
- **Uniform rhythm.** Every sentence the same length, or several in a row opening the same way. Let the content set the length.
- **Em dashes** (`---` and `~---` in LaTeX). Use a period or a comma.
- **Stacked hedges.** "could potentially help to". One hedge at most.

## Resume and cover-letter tells

- **Stock openers.** "I am excited to apply for", "I am writing to express my interest in". Open with the reason: the concrete thing in the posting and how it connects to the author's work.
- **Stock self-descriptions.** "passionate", "results-driven", "proven track record", "team player", "detail-oriented", "I work reliably and independently". Replace them with evidence the reader can check: what was built, for whom, with what result. Test: a sentence that could appear unchanged in another applicant's letter gets rewritten or cut.
- **Inflated verbs.** spearheaded, orchestrated, leveraged, utilized, championed, drove. Use the verb that says what happened: built, led, cut, wrote, ran, moved, used.
- **Buzz vocabulary.** delve, crucial, robust, seamless, cutting-edge, dynamic, landscape, synergy, foster, elevate, harness, showcase. Use the plain word, or the number.
- **Alignment claims.** "My skills align perfectly with your requirements", "I am confident I would be a valuable asset". Show the match with one example and let the reader conclude.
- **Generic closers.** "I look forward to hearing from you", "I would welcome the opportunity to discuss". End with one specific sentence: what the author would like to work on or ask about. A single short courtesy line before the sign-off is fine.
- **Echoed posting.** Keep the posting's names for skills and technologies, since screening software matches them, but write the sentences around them in the author's own words.
- **Bullets.** Start with the verb, end with the result and its number, one line where possible. Keep the bullets of a list in the same form. "Responsible for X" becomes what was done to X.

## Clarity

- One idea per sentence. Split a sentence that runs past about 25 words or needs a semicolon.
- Active voice with the author as the subject: "I cut downtime by 17%", rather than "Downtime was reduced".
- Numbers over adverbs: "significantly faster" becomes the measured figure, when the text or the Profile has one.
- Concrete over abstract: name the system, the customer, the technology.
- Plain words: "use" for utilize, "help" for facilitate, "to" for in order to, "because" for due to the fact that.
- One topic per paragraph, stated in its first sentence.
